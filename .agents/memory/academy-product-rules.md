name: Academy product rules
description: Confirmed product boundaries and privacy choices to preserve across iterations.
---
The user explicitly chose Streamlit and an iPad-first, picture-led experience. Preserve nine sections; Social Skills should be supportive communication tools, never assessments.

**Why:** These were explicit scope choices, not defaults inferred from the scaffold.

**How to apply:** Do not replace Streamlit with React merely because the artifact was scaffolded as a web app.

For Social Skills, do not diagnose, score emotions, or claim certainty about another person's intent. A parent must enable AI help; each recording of another person requires that speaker's disclosed permission. Social interpretation inputs (audio, image, transcript) are not saved by the app; only wording AJ explicitly saves is parent-visible and deletable.

**Why:** The user wants language and conversation support for an 8-year-old while protecting his and other people's privacy.

**How to apply:** Keep low-pressure communication prompts available without AI, keep interpretation temporary by default, and make any saved phrase reviewable and removable by the child and parent.

The parent explicitly chose a reversible, financial-only view of scrambled demo data, not replacement of saved accounts.

**Why:** Original exports, reviewed statements, balances and assumptions must remain available when demo mode is turned off.

**How to apply:** Keep demo imports/read-only examples separate from saved financial evidence. Do not treat financial demo mode as anonymization of the whole app: diary, photos, rewards and other family data remain unchanged.

Reward points carry over indefinitely. Parents set routine points and a default points-per-dollar conversion, with per-item overrides. The arcade is a parent-approved, primarily weekend privilege and never costs points.

**Why:** The user explicitly distinguished earned rewards from access to a game the child built.

**How to apply:** Never reset the balance weekly or charge arcade points. Participation, not emotions or athletic quality, earns points.

Child investment views emphasize percentages and life goals; parents see money amounts and planning assumptions. Diary entries are visible to parents, with clear disclosure.

**Why:** The user requested simplified child-facing finance and parent visibility for every entry.

**How to apply:** Do not expose private financial reports in child views or infer mental-health scores from diary text.

The user explicitly chose password-free entry to the existing family child view, not a sample-data preview; the internal parent portal must remain password-protected.

**Why:** After disclosure that anyone able to reach the app could access child-visible saved content, the user selected “Existing family child view.”

**How to apply:** Do not reintroduce parent approval for child entry or silently substitute synthetic data. Preserve parent-only authorization and do not describe the accessible child view as anonymized. This access policy supersedes the earlier child-session approval requirement.

“Preview Watson” in the initial login request was a dictation misunderstanding, not a new product name.

**Why:** The user clarified that “preview what's on” means showing the available Academy sections.

**How to apply:** Use that clarification over the older literal wording in the entry-screen plan; do not rename the Academy.
